01 · The problem

Why contact-center projects go live and nothing improves.

The technology usually works. The delivery model is what fails — because it measures effort, not improvement.

The traditional implementation

  • Paid on hours, so the incentive is effort, not improvement.
  • Go-live is the finish line. The team rolls off exactly when your agents need the most help.
  • Every requirement gets built, so agents inherit cluttered screens that slow every call down.
  • Success is reported in activity — workshops held, milestones green — while handle time and satisfaction sit unmeasured.

Managed outcomes

  • Paid on verified results. Fees attach to improvements you can see in your own telemetry.
  • Go-live is mile one. We monitor, tune, and improve continuously after launch.
  • Built for the call, not the requirement list — only what makes agents faster and customers happier.
  • Success is reported in your measures — handle time, resolution, satisfaction — on a scoreboard we both see.
Wrong finish line

The old way: value peaks at launch, then decays back to the industry's 1-in-5.

Only 1 in 5 CRM customers ever sees a tangible business outcome. That's not a technology failure — it's what happens when go-live is the finish line. Managed outcomes is the punchline: we stay on the field after launch, and our fee depends on the climb.

02 · The model

What an outcome is — and how we get paid.

Many firms market "outcomes" and bill for hours. The difference is visible in the fee structure, so here is ours.

What we mean by an outcome

An outcome is a specific, measurable improvement in how your contact center performs — agreed with you up front, baselined before we start, and verified through telemetry from your own environment. Technical outcomes get the platform live: modern workspace, unified data, AI assistance in the queue. Business outcomes are why you're doing it: faster resolution, shorter handle time, happier customers.

Business results

What "fees at risk" means here

A portion of our fee is only invoiceable when a milestone verifies. If a milestone is missed, the fee is held, we remediate at our cost, and it's remeasured. Not verified, not invoiced. And the total never inflates — a $1M engagement stays a $1M engagement; what changes is that payment follows proof.

What financial risk stays with you

We're direct about this: business results depend on factors we share — your staffing, your sponsorship, your market. That's why fees attach to the drivers we can verify, while business measures are baselined, tracked, and reported openly — never hidden behind a claim.
